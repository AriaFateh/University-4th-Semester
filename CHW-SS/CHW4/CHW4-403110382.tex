	\begin{itemize}

	\section{Z-Transform}
	\subsection{Moving Average}
	\subsubsection{Theory}
	The N-point moving average filter is defined as:
	\begin{equation}
		y[n] = \frac1N \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} x[n-k]
	\end{equation}
	We take the Z-transform of both sides of the equation.
	\begin{equation}
		Y(z) = \frac1N \sum_{k = 0}^{N-1} z^{-k} X(z)
	\end{equation}
	\begin{equation}
		H(z) = \frac1N \sum_{k = 0}^{N-1} z^{-k} = \frac1N \frac{1-z^{-N}}{1-z^{-1}}
	\end{equation}
	If you input an impulse $\delta[n]$ into the system, the coefficient of the sum dictate that output will be $\frac1N$ for $n = 0, \cdots, N-1$ and $0$ for everywhere else. Because this impulse response has a finite duration, it is by definition an FIR filter.
	
	\subsubsection{Code}
	\begin{lstlisting}
N = [3,7,15];
figure;
for i=1:length(N)
	n = N(i);
	numerator = zeros(1 , n+1);
	denominator = zeros(1,n+1);
	numerator(1) =  1./n;numerator(n+1) = -1./n;
	denominator(1) = 1; denominator(2) = -1;

	subplot(1,length(N),i);
	zplane(numerator,denominator);
end
	\end{lstlisting}
	\subsubsection{Result}
		In the Figure \ref{zplane}, zero-pole plot is shown. Zeros are distributed uniformly around the unit circle. We have a zero and a pole at $z=1$. They perfectly cancel each other out. In reality, the system does not have a pole at $z=1$.
		
		Whenever the unit circle passes through a zero, the frequency response drops completely to zero. Because your zeros are at $\omega = \frac{2\pi k}{N}$, the filter will completely block those specific frequencies (and their harmonics).
		
		The $(N-1)$ poles at $(0,0)$ do not change the magnitude of the frequency response because their distance to any point on the unit circle is always exactly $1$. They only introduce a linear time delay to the signal.
		
		Because the zero at $z=1$ (which corresponds to DC, or $\omega=0$) was canceled by the pole, there is no zero there to attenuate the signal. The magnitude response is at its absolute maximum at $\omega=0$.
		
		In the frequency domain, an $N$-point moving average filter produces a \var{sinc}-like shape (Take a look at Figure \ref{frequency}). Due to the figure, it is a low-pass filter. Increasing \var{N} will produce more zeros in Figure \ref{frequency}(The number of roots is $[N/2]$).
