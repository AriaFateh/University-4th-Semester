	\section{Problem 1}
My name is Aria Fateh Kerdari, a second-year Electrical Engineering student at Sharif University of Technology. People often ask why someone who won two gold medals in Astronomy and Astrophysics—OWAO 2023 in Sochi and IOAA 2024 in Brazil—is studying electrical engineering instead. The truth is, it does not matter to me which specific field I choose as long as it interests me deeply. My journey from mapping stars to designing circuits was never a compromise; it was simply my way of finding things that truly ignite my curiosity. I have learned that I am not defined by these academic achievements, but by my ability to adapt and focus on what makes me excited.

I once believed the universe was my only home. However, I quickly realized that discipline does not require a fixed label. Just as there is no definitive "perfect movie," neither does existence demand that one field be superior to another. 

Music is one of my most passionate hobbies. Playing the piano allows me to experience a space where there are no "best genres" or perfect songs—only moments that touch the soul. Similarly, I do not just watch films for fun; I study them closely, paying attention to every tiny detail directors and writers put in place—from lighting choices to subtle dialogue nuances—to fulfill my creative needs. This habit of looking beyond the surface has trained me to see patterns and complexities that others might miss.

At the World Youth Festival in Russia, surrounded by thousands of young people from 190 nations, my story remains consistent: it is about following what sparks curiosity rather than sticking to one label. Whether I am analyzing stars, playing the piano, or studying the intricate details in movies, my approach stays the same: paying close attention and finding meaning in everything.
